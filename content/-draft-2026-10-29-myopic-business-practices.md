## TLDR

Why do businesses continuously refuse to pay down various forms of "debt"? Because in the short term, its never more valuable than building something new.

## Huh?

Everyone knows about tech debt - those inelegant, hacky, disorganized, duplicative areas of code that make the code harder to understand and maintain. But what about "product debt"? Those small papercuts and inconsistencies you introduce into a product over the years as a result of forcing competing paradigms into a single interface. New products have new requirements, but re-writing the old thing to match the new thing would be too much work, so we just shove the new thing next to the old thing and say we'll come back later to reconcile the two.

Product debt is often taken on in the same way tech debt is taken on: as an intentional choice, a tradeoff taken to deliver faster. Neither tech debt nor product debt is paid down easily. They both take intention and focus. But in my experience, paying down tech debt tends to happen more naturally, because engineers are more likely to "just fix it" when they are finally fed up with the horrors of working in a dilapidated codebase. Product debt gets no such love, because fixing product debt means getting external buy-in. If you want to consolidate two similar-but-different interfaces, you have to get Product approval. If you want to align some design tokens across the site, you have to get Product approval. If you want to remove duplicative screens, you have to get Product approval. That extra friction means it never gets done.

We acknowledge when we're "kicking the can down the road", but we never plan to pick up the can. We hope a crow finds it and thinks its shiney enough to justify taking it.

Organizations are continuously myopic because they are always focused on delivering initiatives for the next quarter. Given that motivation, paying down product debt will **never** make sense because it is **never** valuable for that specific quarter's deliverables. Nobody gets bonus points for making things better for the next person. They get evaluated on whether or not they hit the targets that were set for them for the previous 3 months.
